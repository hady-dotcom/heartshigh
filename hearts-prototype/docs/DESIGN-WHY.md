# HEARTS: why each screen exists (from Leon's deck, 2026-10-04)

Every agent must read this before changing a learner screen. Keep each screen's reason. This is a basis, not a rulebook: a better idea from testing can replace a specific in the deck. Where a newer decision from Leon conflicts, the newer one wins, and that is noted below.

## Page 1. Hors d'oeuvre (first screen from Home; 15–20 s clip)

Why it works:

- It meets people where they already are, in the short-form video market.
- The saying does the hooking, and the kinetic text reinforces it.
- Four swipes, no menus: replay, more on this topic, more from this speaker, or switch lanes. Every swipe loops to another taste. Gestures must be hinted. The live feed shows ‘↑ swipe up to replay’.
- Sharing is built in (Share and Save on the right).
- One clear call to action. Never several competing buttons. On a playing clip that button is ‘Learn more’, and it steps up to the next level.
- Lane chip (‘Lane · Patience’), a timer that counts, and a speaker bar with Follow.

## Page 2. Appetiser (30 s to 3 min, still whole-screen)

- A longer commitment without a change of feel: same full-screen look as the hors d'oeuvre.
- Interest piqued for more.
- The teacher's presence is doubled (bigger speaker card, which opens the speaker page).
- A visible level label that reads as a small step up. Learners see ‘Ready for more?’ (Leon, 2026-10-04). That replaces the deck's ‘Extended cut’ chip, and the same words are used on the feed chip, the course page row, the garden row, and the toast when there is no further clip at that step.
- One CTA, still ‘Learn more’, which opens the full talk. Elapsed time shows against the length of the cut.

## Page 3. Speaker page (opens from the speaker bar)

- A second door into the courses, for more engagement.
- A real bio builds trust.
- A low-commitment intro from the speaker (‘Watch intro’).
- One tap to the curriculum: the speaker's courses listed with part counts and Start.
- Follow and ‘Ask a question’.

## Page 4. Mains: the course (course player, engagement points, My Garden)

- Watching becomes developing.
- Engagement points sit on the timeline (the dots), and one CTA says which point is next (‘Answer question N →’). That is the same job as the deck's ‘Answer point N →’.
- Swarm learning: ‘What others said’ sits under the question once the learner has answered. Sharing one's own answer stays opt-in.
- Progress you can see literally bearing fruit. The garden card shows the fruit count, a progress bar, and ‘↗ Part N · Next’ whenever the course has another part.
- Responses are feedback for the sheikhs' teams (so questions must be worth a sheikh reading).
- The question sheet slides up over the paused video, with a way to write or record, a private switch, and a share control.

## Newer decisions that override the deck

- This note is a basis, not a rulebook. Keep the reason. Testing can replace a label, a layout detail, or a button name.
- Colours: the evening-garden look (deep teal, warm parchment, gold), not the pale cream in the original mocks.
- The middle level is labelled ‘Ready for more?’ for learners. Staff desks still say appetiser.
- Mains must be real long talks. Opening a main first shows the whole course (every talk, counts, total time, ‘Plan the rest of this course’ when there are two or more parts), and questions only appear inside the video.
- Hors d'oeuvres and appetisers never count towards course completion or the garden.
- Wide video on phones: small and uncropped on top with typography below, never centre-crop, and never tell learners to turn the phone.
