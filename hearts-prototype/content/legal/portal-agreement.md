# Running HEARTS in your community

**Draft for adviser review.** This page is the agreement between a masjid or school and HEARTS. It is not legal advice. An adviser must review it.

## In a line

You decide who joins and who looks after them. HEARTS hosts the app and protects the data.

## What you do (the community)

You are the community that runs this portal. You choose the access codes, the teachers, and who may join. You name a privacy contact and a safeguarding lead before learners arrive. You look after the people, including children whose families you already know.

If you collect parental consent on paper at the madrasah, you tick that on the Children page and record each child there. HEARTS will not pretend a grown-up has agreed until you do.

## What HEARTS does (the host)

HEARTS provides the app, the servers and the locks. In data-protection words that an adviser will recognise: the portal is closer to the people (a controller for “who is in this class”); HEARTS is closer to the hosting (a processor for the store, and a controller for the shared library and the master desk).

HEARTS will not sell the portal’s data, will not use learner words to advertise, and will not use them to train a model where the contract with the AI provider forbids it.

## Children

If your portal will have anyone under 13, you either collect a grown-up’s agreement through HEARTS, or you switch on “the school collects consent offline” and tick each child. Child-safe defaults stay on for everyone under 18.

## When something is wrong

You handle day-to-day care: a kind word, a hidden post, a paused account. HEARTS will tell you if the host itself must act (a broken server, a lost bucket, a wipe).

## Ending

You can ask HEARTS to pause the portal. After a pause, the master is asked whether to keep or wipe. Learners’ reflections are not a thing to throw away lightly.

## The tick

By naming yourself below and ticking the box, you confirm that you have read this page and that your community will look after the people who join.
