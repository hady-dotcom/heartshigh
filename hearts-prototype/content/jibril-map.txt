A Curriculum from One Sitting
Forty-one units from Hadith Jibril. Three Ghunya seats under each. Thirty
lecture series hung by heading. Videos in, around, and against.

The hadith is still the course. The five-volume English Ghunya (Holland / Al-Baz, 2008) is
still the provision. What has changed is the join: not one heading per clause, and not a thin
line of forty-one lessons. Each clause now opens three seats in the book. The book’s pages
are counted, then asked where they best sit. The thirty lecture series (standalone skipped)
hang under a unit by the language of the heading. Transcripts come later for a bang-on fit.
A series may sit under more than one clause when the title talks to both — Maryam under
Books first, then angels and Jibril.

 [Source] page numbers are the printed Al-Baz pagination from each volume’s contents. [Designer]
 is the hang and the seating. Five English volumes are an editorial split, not year-groups. Key
 Stages and hours are not al-Jīlānī’s.

2. Best sit, not the slope
The earlier 41-unit map used a designer slope: Sitting leans Volume 1 adab; Islam leans the
Door; Iman leans Chapter 4; Ihsan leans Volumes 2–3; Hour leans Volume 3; the trunk leans
Volume 4 then 5. That is pacing. It is not where the pages actually live.
If each Ghunya block is given one primary home — where the matter best sits, allowing
later grain to return — the English pages fall like this. Overlap is refused in the totals so the
book still adds to 1,660. A block can still be read again under a later unit; it is not counted
twice.

What this does to the curriculum. Ihsan and the Hour (as lived time, not a signs
syllabus) eat about half the book. Islam is a fifth, and most of that fifth is Volume 4’s prayer,
not the Door. Iman is about a tenth. The sitting is larger than iman because there are two
books of manners. The last line of the hadith, if it is only leftover pages, is almost nothing.
The last line only becomes a trunk if you reassign Volume 4 and Volume 5 to it — which is
the slope, not the best-sit. Both views are true; they answer different questions.
Unit 41 cannot swallow Volume 4 then Volume 5 as one lesson (398 + 156 = 554 English
pages, a third of the book). Three seats under each earlier clause is how that mass is
already being read, so the trunk is a return, not a first dump.

4. Three seats under each part
Not one-for-one. Under every clause: the teaching (your hang), then three Ghunya seats,
then the lecture series whose heading belongs here. A seat can be a later grain of the same
act. “Thin” means the book has the idea, not a chapter of that name. IVF is your hang,
labelled; the Ghunya has no such chapter.

5. Videos — in, around, against
Looked for, not invented. “In” teaches with the clause. “Around” is adjacent (same author,
same hadith, neighbouring adab). “Against” pulls the other way: a flattening, a checklist, a
different book sold as this one, a method the Ghunya does not use. Watch against as a foil,
not as the lesson. I did not find a dedicated English video series on this Ghunya; Jilani on
video is almost always a different book. IVF: no matching video found; that hang stays
yours.

6. What this means for the course
Stop limiting one-for-one. Forty-one clauses stay. Each now has three furnishings, so a fat
block (five prayers, Ramadan, tawba, the calendar) can sit under more than one hang
without being a forced title.
Think in paper. On the 700-scale, a “unit” is not 700 ÷ 41 ≈ 17 pages. Ihsan is about 209 of
those 700. The Hour-as-lived-year is about 156. Islam is about 145, mostly Volume 4. The
sitting is about 110. Iman is about 76. The trunk is either 4 leftover pages or the return of a
third of the book. That choice is the curriculum.
The slope is still the order you walk. Best-sit is where you send people into the book when a
clause opens. Videos are a third room: in, around, against — so the course is not only Jīlānī,
and not whatever YouTube flattened the hadith into.
